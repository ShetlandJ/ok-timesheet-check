# The Monday morning timesheet check

A six-sheet click-through, built for two of the finance staff at Ocean Kinetics Ltd
(Lerwick) after an "Working with AI" seminar that was pitched over their heads. It
walks through one job they actually do:

1. **Two records of the same week** — the timesheet typed up by hand, and the door
   scanner's log of when the men actually arrived and left.
2. **Just ask the question** — drop both files in and ask in plain English. Then the
   catch: you have to remember to do it, every week.
3. **The answer** — who agrees, who doesn't, and what's simply missing.
4. **Ask once. Get it every week.** — the same job asked for as a standing routine.
5. **What it does with that** — the five steps, unattended.
6. **Monday, 06:42** — the report already in the inbox.

Self-contained single HTML file, no build step. Arrow keys, space, or click anywhere
to advance; right-click to go back. Print to PDF for a handout — one sheet per page.

## The data is invented

Every name, time and figure is made up, and it says so on sheet 1. The face scanner
at Ocean Kinetics is real; the two neat files in this story are not — the whole thing
is "imagine these two datasets existed". The numbers do at least reconcile properly:
a nominal 07:00–17:00 day with half an hour off for dinner Monday to Thursday, a short
Friday, 222.0 hours written down against 211.7 hours scanned.

Just ask the question

Drag both files in. Type what you want in the words you’d use to ask a colleague. No formulas, no lookups, no macros — and nothing to build first.

✱Good morning, Tara

Here’s the timesheet I typed up for last week and the export from the door scanner for the same five men. Do the hours they wrote down match the hours they actually scanned in for? Take half an hour off each full day for dinner. Show me anyone more than half an hour out either way, and tell me if anything’s missing.

You don’t format the question

Long, rambling, in your own words. Exactly how you’d say it across the office.

You set the rules

Half an hour for dinner. Half an hour of tolerance. Your rules, not its guesses.

You ask for the gaps too

“Tell me if anything’s missing” is the important half. A blank is not a zero.

The catch

Nothing wrong with any of that — it took under a minute. But you have to remember it’s Monday, you have to go and find both files, and you have to type it again. Fifty-two times a year, on the morning you’re busiest. Miss a week and nobody notices.

Sheet 4 · The automatic way

Ask once. Get it every week.

That was useful — but you had to remember to do it, find both files, and type the question again. So don’t ask the question. Ask for the routine.

Every Monday at 07:00, take last week’s timesheet and the door-scanner export out of the shared folder and do that same comparison. Email me the differences before I get in — I start at seven, so I want it sitting there when I take my coat off. Flag anyone more than half an hour out either way, and anyone with a scan missing. If it all matches, still send it and just say so. If one of the files isn’t there, tell me — don’t guess.

Sheet 5 · What happens while you’re driving in

What it does with that

Nobody opens anything. Nobody clicks a button. That one paragraph is now a standing instruction, and this is the whole of it:

01

Wakes up

Monday, 06:40. On its own. Your laptop can be shut and in the boot.

02

Fetches

Last week’s timesheet and last week’s scan export, out of the shared folder.

03

Does the sums

Matches the names, adds up every scan pair, takes the dinner breaks off, compares.

04

Writes it up

Plain English, worst first. Missing records kept separate from real differences.

05

Emails you

Straight to your inbox. Before 07:00, every Monday, until you tell it to stop.

Set up once. The only thing that changes week to week is the two files. If they’re not there, it says so rather than inventing numbers.
